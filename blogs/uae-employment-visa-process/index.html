1. Overview of the UAE Employment Visa

Sponsoring an international employee in the UAE is one of the most critical legal milestones for any business. Whether you run a mainland LLC registered with MoHRE or a free zone entity in Dubai, Sharjah, or Abu Dhabi, every non-citizen worker requires an official residence visa tied to your corporate sponsorship.

The standard employment visa is valid for two years (for mainland entities and most free zones) and grants the holder legal residency, the right to open bank accounts, lease property, and sponsor their family dependents in the UAE.

2. Employer Prerequisites Before Applying

Before initiating an application on the government portals, your company must possess valid corporate credentials:

  • Active Trade Licence: Mainland licence registered with the Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) or relevant Free Zone authority.
  • Immigration Establishment Card: Issued by the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA) or ICP.
  • MoHRE Corporate File: For mainland companies, an active file without pending fines or WPS blocks.
  • Available Visa Quota: Sufficient electronic visa quota allocated to your commercial facility.

3. Step 1: Document Collection & Pre-Screening

90% of visa rejections and delays occur because of incomplete or incorrectly formatted documentation. As an employer, you must assemble:

  • Clear passport copy (must have at least 6 months validity remaining).
  • Passport-sized photograph with pure white background (matching ICP digital photo specifications).
  • Attested educational certificates: Required for Skill Levels 1 to 3 (managerial and specialised professional positions). Certificates must be attested by the UAE Embassy in the home country and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) in the UAE.
  • Signed standard UAE offer letter (or Free Zone standard employment contract).

4. Step 2: Work Permit & Entry Permit Application

Once documents are verified, the application is submitted through MoHRE (for mainland) or the Free Zone portal:

  1. Work Permit Approval: MoHRE issues electronic quota approval and work permit clearance.
  2. Entry Permit Issuance: Immigration issues the official e-Visa (pink slip). If the candidate is currently outside the UAE, they use this entry permit to enter the country.
  3. Status Change (Inside UAE): If the candidate is already in the UAE on a visit visa or previous cancelled visa, an in-country "Change of Status" is filed without them needing to exit the country.

5. Step 3: Medical Fitness Examination

Within 30 days of status change or arrival, the applicant must complete the mandatory UAE health screening at a government-certified preventive medicine centre (DHA in Dubai or EHS in Sharjah/Northern Emirates):

  • Blood test (screening for communicable diseases such as HIV and Hepatitis).
  • Chest X-ray (screening for active pulmonary tuberculosis).

Standard medical test results are available within 24 to 48 hours, while VIP 4-hour express services are available at select centres.

6. Step 4: Emirates ID Biometrics Registration

Concurrently with the medical test, an official application for the physical Emirates ID card is submitted via the ICP portal:

First-time applicants must visit an ICP customer happiness centre to record their fingerprints, facial biometric scans, and signature. Returning residents who have previously registered biometrics can often complete this step digitally.

7. Step 5: Residency Visa Stamping & Emirates ID Delivery

Upon receipt of the medical fitness certificate, the final residency permit application is submitted. Under modern UAE regulations, the physical visa sticker has been replaced by the official digital residence permit, and the physical Emirates ID card serves as the primary proof of residency throughout the country.

The physical Emirates ID card is printed and dispatched via Emirates Post or designated express couriers to your registered company address.

8. Timelines & Avoiding Costly Delays

From initial document collection to physical card delivery, a standard employment visa typically takes between two to four weeks. However, delays can occur due to:

  • Non-attested educational certificates for professional designations.
  • Mismatched job titles between the commercial licence and MoHRE classification.
  • Overstaying previous tourist or cancelled employment visas resulting in daily immigration fines.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Legally, an employee can begin their onboarding duties once the electronic MoHRE work permit and entry permit have been formally issued, provided the status change has been completed.

Under UAE Labour Law, the employer is legally obligated to bear all recruitment, visa processing, medical examination, and Emirates ID fees. Passing these costs onto the employee or deducting them from their salary is strictly illegal.
